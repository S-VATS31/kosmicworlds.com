Our Mission

Train and validate autonomous systems before they reach the real world.

We are building world models that give robotics and autonomous-driving teams more ways to learn from experience, train policies, and test decisions in simulation.

Explore our models ↗

What we’re building toward

Simulate experience before deployment.

Simulation can expand the situations a system encounters during development. Our goal is to make those experiences useful for both learning and evaluation.

01 / Training

Learn across more scenarios.

Generate varied experiences paired with physical actions, giving teams data for training driving policies and robot controllers.

Explore changes in environments, motion, and operating conditions without collecting every variation individually.

02 / Validation

Test how a system responds.

Use closed-loop simulation to study how a policy’s actions change what happens next.

Revisit scenarios, vary conditions, and compare behavior to understand where a system succeeds and where it needs more work.

Research priorities

What useful simulation requires.

Our research connects generated observations with the controls, time horizons, and physical behavior that matter to autonomous systems.

  1. 01

    Actions with physical meaning.

    Steering, throttle, and brake for driving. Joint commands for robotics. The aim is to condition a rollout on controls that a policy can produce and a machine can execute.

  2. 02

    Consistency over longer horizons.

    A useful simulation needs to retain scene information as a sequence unfolds. We focus on preserving geometry, objects, and state across longer rollouts.

  3. 03

    Physical grounding.

    The relationship between an action and its outcome needs to be credible. We work toward generated motion and observations that remain consistent with physical constraints.
