Method

Control labels from
driving video.

PACE uses a proprietary physics-based model to estimate control inputs from egocentric driving video. The resulting action labels are synchronized with the video for model training.

    Egocentric video

  3. 03 / Output

    Frame-aligned controls

    • Steering wheel angle radians
    • Throttle position 0–100%
    • Brake position 0–100%

Evaluation scope and calibration notes

One front-camera timeline is counted per dataset: Udacity’s center camera, RoboBus camera 200, and synchronized Yaak state rows counted once. Original finite-value, duplicate, direction, and camera-alignment masks are preserved.

  • Coverage: above 5 m/s, valid predictions cover 98.66% of eligible RoboBus frames and 99.996% of Udacity frames; the other six datasets have 100% coverage under their eligibility masks.
  • A2D2 and CONOPS: A2D2 covers the available continuous-motion records. CONOPS covers Port Queuing recordings; its cross-country release has no comparable steering target.
  • Yaak: steering errors in degrees inherit the adapter’s ±480° conversion from normalized steering.
  • Turing Japan: the retained effective steering ratio was calibrated previously, and calibration recordings are included in the reported evaluation. This is not a wholly held-out calibration benchmark.

The public steering benchmark scores frames above 5 m/s. Selected demonstration excerpts below have a different scope. Detailed speed breakdowns are available in the full partner report.

Scope and limitations

Where accuracy
depends on context.

Low-speed and stopped steering

Small motion errors are amplified at low speed. A stationary trajectory cannot reveal steering while stopped. Parking and reversing remain important open cases. Detailed lower-speed analysis is available in the full partner report.

Vehicle-specific accuracy

Accuracy varies across vehicles and operating conditions. Results for known evaluation vehicles do not guarantee equivalent performance on an unknown vehicle.

Heavy and articulated vehicles

The current dynamics model treats the vehicle as one rigid body. The coach and truck results do not validate trailer articulation or a general multi-body model.

Demonstration overlays

Steering paths are approximate projections for visualization, not measured future trajectories. Unsupported channels are omitted or marked N/A. The demonstrations use real dataset footage with control overlays.
